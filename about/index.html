About

Threat Intel Specialist is a free, independent briefing on cybersecurity for anyone who wants to know what is going on, whether or not they work in the field.

Who runs it

Threat Intel Specialist is run by Laxminarayana Ganesh, a cybersecurity specialist with more than 18 years' experience protecting critical sectors such as energy and utilities. Laxmi holds the CISSP and CCSP certifications and is certified in IEC 62443, the standard for securing industrial control systems.

“I built this site to keep track of cybersecurity incidents and the latest cyber news in one place.”

The site is independent. It carries no advertising, takes no sponsorship, and no vendor pays to appear here.

What is on the site

  • Today: a short briefing on the last day and week.
  • News: headlines from security journalists, researchers and government agencies.
  • Vulnerabilities: flaws attackers are using now, and the week's new serious ones.
  • Incidents: attacks and breaches as they are reported, with a dashboard.
  • History: the full record of tracked incidents, with trends and lessons.
  • Blog and Learn: plain-language guides and a glossary.

Where the data comes from

Everything here comes from public sources. The site checks them every 30 minutes.

  • News comes from the public feeds of security news sites, research teams and government agencies. Only the headline and a short excerpt are shown, with a link to the original article.
  • Incidents come from three places: press reports found in those news feeds; Ransomware.live, a public tracker of attacks and of victims named on ransomware leak sites; and an archive of 2025 leak-site claims compiled from BreachSense listings.
  • Exploited vulnerabilities come from the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue published by CISA.
  • New vulnerabilities come from the US National Vulnerability Database (NVD). This product uses data from the NVD but is not endorsed or certified by the NVD.
  • Likelihood of attack scores come from EPSS, published by FIRST.

    How incidents are labelled

    Reports about the same event are grouped into one entry. Each entry carries one of five evidence labels, so a claim is never presented as a fact:

    • Confirmed: the affected organisation, a regulator, a court or a government body has acknowledged the incident.
    • Corroborated: at least two independent, reputable sources report it, but no official acknowledgement has been found.
    • Reported: one reputable source reports it, with no official confirmation yet.
    • Claimed: an attacker or a leak site says it happened. This is an early warning, not proof. Most entries on this site are claims.
    • Disputed: the organisation denies or challenges the allegation.

    A label can strengthen as better evidence appears, and each entry keeps its source trail. Labels are assigned by software reading headlines and excerpts, with help from an AI model when one is available, and entries that used AI say so. This process makes mistakes. Treat each entry as a pointer to its sources, not as a finding of fact. The methodology page has the detail.

    Lessons and summaries

    The incident history shows lessons for each entry. Where an AI model has read the report, the lessons are its suggestions based on what the report says. Everywhere else they are general advice for that type of incident, and are marked as general. Neither kind is a finding about what the affected organisation did or did not do.

    Reading the trends

    The charts describe what this site has collected, not everything that happened. Ransomware claims are over-represented because leak sites publish them in bulk, coverage before 2025 is thin, many entries have no industry recorded, and incidents that are never made public do not appear at all.

    Corrections

    If an entry is wrong, out of date, or names your organisation unfairly, open an issue on GitHub with a link to the entry and a source. Corrections are applied by hand and take priority over the automatic labels.

    Limits

    This site is for general information. It is not professional security, legal or financial advice, and it is not a complete record of every incident or vulnerability. Check the linked sources before acting on anything here.

    Privacy

    The site uses Google Analytics to count visits. It has no accounts, no comments and no advertising trackers. Your theme choice is stored only in your browser.
